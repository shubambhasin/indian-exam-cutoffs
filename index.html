Four things that make these numbers easy to misread

Qualifying is not admission

A qualifying cutoff only gets a candidate into counselling. The mark that actually wins a seat — the closing rank — is far higher, and differs by institute and branch. Both are shown here, separately and never mixed.

Five categories rarely mean five lines

NEET-UG has two distinct bars, not five: EWS sits inside the UR band, and OBC, SC and ST share the 40th percentile. JEE Advanced has three. Charts here draw only the bands that genuinely differ.

A falling cutoff is not an easier exam

The JAB lowers JEE Advanced criteria until about twice the available seats qualify; NBEMS cuts NEET-PG mid-counselling when seats go vacant. These lines track seat supply and policy at least as much as candidate performance.

Reserved ranks are category ranks

In JoSAA data an ST closing rank of 16 is a rank among ST candidates only — not an All India Rank. Comparing it directly against an OPEN rank of 66 is meaningless. NEET closing ranks, by contrast, are All India Ranks.

Exam by exam

Each record shows the qualifying bar over time, the rule behind it, and what the series cannot tell you. Gaps are left visible rather than interpolated.

Benchmark closing ranks

Two reference points for what a seat actually cost, as opposed to what clearing the exam cost. Coverage here is thinner than for qualifying cutoffs — counselling authorities publish per-round data that is rarely archived in comparable form.

Sources

Primary sources are documents published by the conducting authority itself and read directly. Secondary sources are coaching and admissions aggregators, used only where no primary document was reachable, and flagged as such on every row they support.

Known gaps. INI-SS has no usable public category series. NExT has never been conducted. NEET-MDS is missing 2019–2021, 2023 and 2024 at category level; GPAT is missing 2021–2024; benchmark closing ranks start in 2024. These are recorded as gaps in the dataset rather than filled with estimates.

Corrections welcome. Every number lives in a single JSON file with the source that supports it. If a figure here disagrees with an official notification, the notification is right — open an issue or a pull request against data/cutoffs.json.
